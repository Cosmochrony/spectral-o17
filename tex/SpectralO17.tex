  \begin{abstract}
    \vspace{0.5em}
    This is a corrective revision of the version published as v1.0.
    That draft treated O16's original finite-window baseline
    ($\dhat_{\mathrm{exact}}\approx3.72\pm0.06$,
    $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}\approx7.44$, withdrawn as unsourced) as
    established, and an identification of this paper's own toy
    Weil-block model with the O12/O13 pipeline, not established here or
    anywhere in this paper's argument.
    Claims resting on that identification --- that this paper ``closes the
    logical gap left open in O16,'' that conjugate pairs are ``the fibres
    of~$\Pi$ as measured by the real pipeline,'' and the consequent
    requalification of O14--O15 --- are withdrawn
    (Section~\ref{sec:why_pair}).

    What survives is self-contained.
    Theorem~\ref{thm:identity} (restricted form) is confirmed.
    Corollary~\ref{cor:structural_equiv} (arbitrary blocks) is
    strengthened: the original proof asserted, without arguing, that the
    dynamics are independent of $e_{b_1}$'s labelling, a step that does
    not go through as stated.
    This revision proves something stronger instead --- exact equality of
    raw redundancy counts for \emph{any two blocks whatsoever}, needing no
    conjugation argument --- which strengthens the claim but undercuts
    its use: an identity holding between non-conjugate blocks says
    nothing specific about $c\leftrightarrow q-c$, and cannot support any
    claim about which blocks share a fibre.
    The same result gives an unconditional replacement for the claim
    about the amplitude factor $r(c,q)$: since the raw count is provably
    block-independent, any block-dependence a normalised observable
    exhibits must come entirely from its normalisation --- replacing an
    unreproducible numeric example with a short proof, scoped to this
    paper's own model.

    No claim about the physical relevance of conjugate pairs, the fibre
    structure of~$\Pi$, or the requalification of O14--O15 survives this
    revision.
  \end{abstract}

  \section{The open problem after O16}
  \label{sec:problem}

  The original draft of this paper (v1.0) opened from O16's original
  claims: a finite-window baseline
  $\dhat_{\mathrm{exact}} \approx 3.72 \pm 0.06$, a doubled exponent
  $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}} \approx 7.44$, and a proposition that conjugate
  sigma-profiles differ by an integer factor $r(c,q)\in\{1,2,3,4\}$,
  attributed to the O12/O13 pipeline~\cite{Beau2026a16,Beau2026a17}.
  O16's own corrective revision has since withdrawn all three: the
  baseline is untraceable to any table in the current O12--O15 texts;
  the $r(c,q)\in\{1,2,3,4\}$ proposition would contradict O12's own
  proved exact fact $\Sigma_n^{(c)}=\Sigma_n^{(-c)}$ if O16's $\sigma_c(n)$
  were the pipeline's $\Sigma_n^{(c_1,c_2,c_3)}$, an identification O16
  itself no longer asserts; and the citation below to ``O15
  Corollary~4.8'' invoked an unconditional no-go that O15's own
  corrective revision found false as originally stated, replacing it
  with a narrower form (specific dynamical weights, hypothesis since
  verified at one of the five tested primes, $q=151$, still open at the
  other four, bounding against $\dhat_{\mathrm{exact}}$ rather
  than the withdrawn $3.72\pm0.06$).
  None of these three numbers is used or needed below.

  What O16 did establish, and what motivates this paper's own toy model,
  is a \emph{question}: O16 proposed a pair-level observable
  $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n) := \sigma_c(n)\,\sigma_{q-c}(n)$ built from
  the exact conjugation identity $\rho_{q-c}=\overline{\rho_c}$, but
  explicitly acknowledged that the identification of conjugate pairs with
  the \emph{fibres of~$\Pi$} was a structurally motivated hypothesis, not
  a derived result.
  Two narrower, purely mathematical questions were left open about the
  raw dynamics underlying that proposal:

  \begin{itemize}
    \item[(Q1)] Why is the raw Gram-Schmidt dynamics identical for
    $\rho_c$ and $\rho_{q-c}$?
    Is this a consequence of $\rho_{q-c} = \overline{\rho_c}$,
    or does it require additional structure?

    \item[(Q2)] What is the structural origin of the
    constant~$r(c,q)$ satisfying
    $\sigma_{q-c}(n) = r(c,q)\,\sigma_c(n)$ for all $n \geq 1$?
    Is it an arithmetic invariant of $(c,q)$, or a pipeline artefact?
  \end{itemize}

  The present paper answers both questions, but only within its own
  explicitly defined toy model (Section~\ref{sec:identity}), not for the
  real O12/O13 pipeline.
  The original draft's claim that this closes O16's logical gap and
  derives ``the observable class dictated by~$\Pi$'' is withdrawn
  (Section~\ref{sec:why_pair}): as Section~\ref{sec:identity} shows, the
  answer to (Q1)--(Q2) in this toy model turns out to hold for
  \emph{any} two blocks, conjugate or not, which is too strong a fact to
  say anything specific about the involution $c \leftrightarrow q-c$ that
  motivates O16's fibre proposal.

  \section{Identity of raw Gram-Schmidt dynamics}
  \label{sec:identity}

  \subsection{Setup}

    Let $q$ be an odd prime.
    The Weil representation $\rho_c$ of $\Heis(\ZqZ)$ at central
    character~$c \in \Fq$ acts on $\mathbb{C}^q$ by:
    \begin{equation}
      [\rho_c(a,b,t)\,\psi](x) \;=\; \omega^{c(bx + t)}\,\psi(x-a),
      \qquad \omega = e^{2\pi i/q}.
      \label{eq:weil_def}
    \end{equation}
    A block in \emph{this paper's own toy model} --- not shown to
    coincide with the real O12/O13 pipeline's actual block structure, a
    generic triple $(c_1,c_2,c_3)$ with frequency-domain fingerprints
    (Section~\ref{sec:why_pair}) --- is specified by a triple
    $(c, b_1, b_2) \in \Fq \times (\ZqZ)^2$, with initial fingerprint
    vector:
    \begin{equation}
      v_0(c, b_1, b_2) \;:=\; \rho_c(b_1, b_2, 0)\,e_0
      \;=\; \omega^{c\,b_2 b_1}\,e_{b_1},
      \label{eq:v0}
    \end{equation}
    where $e_k$ denotes the $k$-th standard basis vector of $\mathbb{C}^q$.

    The Gram-Schmidt redundancy count at BFS shell~$n$ for this block is:
    \begin{multline}
      \tilde\sigma_c(n;\, b_1, b_2)
      \;:=\; \bigl|\bigl\{g \in \mathrm{Shell}_n :
      \rho_c(g)\,v_0(c,b_1,b_2) \\
      \in \mathrm{span}\bigl\{\rho_c(h)\,v_0 : h \in \mathrm{Shell}_{<n}\bigr\}\bigr\}\bigr|.
      \label{eq:raw_sigma}
    \end{multline}
    This is the \emph{raw} count: the number of Weil images at shell~$n$
    that are already in the Gram-Schmidt span accumulated over
    shells~$0, \ldots, n-1$.

  \subsection{Main results}

    The analysis splits into two levels: an exact equality under a support
    condition, and a structural isomorphism for arbitrary block parameters.

    \begin{theorem}[Identity of raw dynamics, restricted form]
      \label{thm:identity}
      Let $q$ be an odd prime and $c \in \Fq$.
      Suppose the two blocks share the same initial support, i.e.\ $b_1 = b_1'$.
      Then:
      \begin{equation}
        \tilde\sigma_{q-c}(n;\, b_1, b_2')
        \;=\; \tilde\sigma_c(n;\, b_1, b_2)
        \qquad \text{for all } n \geq 0.
        \label{eq:raw_identity}
      \end{equation}
    \end{theorem}

    \begin{proof}
      From equation~\eqref{eq:v0}, both initial vectors have support $\{b_1\}$:
      \[
        v_0(c,b_1,b_2) = \omega^{c\,b_2 b_1}\,e_{b_1},
        \qquad
        v_0(q-c,b_1,b_2') = \omega^{(q-c)\,b_2' b_1}\,e_{b_1}.
      \]
      These differ only by an overall unimodular scalar.
      The conjugation identity $\rho_{q-c} = \overline{\rho_c}$ gives,
      for every $g \in \Heis(\ZqZ)$:
      \[
        \rho_{q-c}(g)\,v_0(q-c,b_1,b_2')
        \;=\; \lambda\,\overline{\rho_c(g)\,v_0(c,b_1,b_2)},
      \]
      where $\lambda \in \mathbb{C}$ is a phase independent of~$g$.
      Since Gram-Schmidt orthogonalisation depends only on the
      linear independence structure of vectors, and since complex
      conjugation preserves this structure
      ($|\langle \bar u, \bar v \rangle| = |\langle u, v \rangle|$),
      the redundancy count of
      $\{\rho_{q-c}(g)\,v_0 : g \in \mathrm{Shell}_{\leq n}\}$
      equals that of
      $\{\rho_c(g)\,v_0 : g \in \mathrm{Shell}_{\leq n}\}$
      at every shell.
      Moreover, complex conjugation preserves linear independence relations
      over $\mathbb{C}$, ensuring that the span structure generated by the
      group orbit is unchanged: a set of vectors is linearly independent
      if and only if its complex conjugate is.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[Scope of Theorem~\ref{thm:identity} --- corrected]
      \label{rem:scope}
      The original draft stated that, for differing initial supports, the
      two dynamics remain isomorphic but ``pointwise equality of counts
      may fail.''
      This is incorrect: Corollary~\ref{cor:structural_equiv} below
      establishes exact pointwise equality even when $b_1 \neq b_1'$, for
      a reason independent of the conjugation identity used in
      Theorem~\ref{thm:identity}'s proof.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{corollary}[Block-independence of the raw redundancy count --- strengthened]
      \label{cor:structural_equiv}
      For \emph{any} two blocks $(c,b_1,b_2)$ and $(c',b_1',b_2')$ with
      $c,c' \in \Fq$ arbitrary (not necessarily conjugate) and
      $(b_1,b_2),(b_1',b_2') \in (\ZqZ)^2$ arbitrary,
      \begin{equation}
        \tilde\sigma_{c'}(n;\,b_1',b_2') \;=\; \tilde\sigma_c(n;\,b_1,b_2)
        \qquad \text{exactly, for all } n \geq 0.
        \label{eq:block_indep}
      \end{equation}
      In particular $\delta_{c'} = \delta_c$ for any $c,c'$, not only for
      conjugate pairs.
      The original draft's version of this statement (restricted to
      $c'=q-c$, and only up to a permutation of generator ordering) is
      superseded by this stronger, unconditional form.
    \end{corollary}

    \begin{proof}
      From equation~\eqref{eq:weil_def}, for any character $\gamma\in\Fq$
      and any $k\in\ZqZ$,
      \[
        \rho_\gamma(a,b,t)\,e_k \;=\; \omega^{\gamma(b(a+k)+t)}\,e_{a+k}:
      \]
      applying $\rho_\gamma(a,b,t)$ to the indicator $e_k$ produces a
      \emph{single} basis vector, supported at $a+k$, scaled by a
      unimodular phase.
      By induction on word length (using the Heisenberg group law, whose
      first coordinate is additive: the first coordinate of $g\cdot g'$ is
      the sum of the first coordinates of $g$ and $g'$), for any
      $g=(a,b,t)$ reachable in the Cayley graph,
      \[
        \rho_\gamma(g)\,v_0(\gamma,k,b_2)
        \;=\; \varphi(g,\gamma,k,b_2)\;e_{a+k}
      \]
      for some unimodular $\varphi$ depending on $g,\gamma,k,b_2$ but
      \emph{not} affecting which basis vector is produced: the support is
      exactly $a+k \bmod q$, determined by $g$'s own first coordinate $a$
      and the shift $k$, and is independent of $\gamma$ and of $b_2$
      entirely.
      Every vector in the orbit $\{\rho_\gamma(g)v_0 : g\in\mathrm{Shell}_{\leq n}\}$
      is therefore a nonzero scalar multiple of a standard basis vector.
      Gram-Schmidt redundancy for a family of vectors that are each
      (nonzero scalar)$\times$(basis vector) depends only on which basis
      indices repeat: two such vectors are linearly dependent iff they
      share the same index, and linearly independent iff they do not,
      regardless of their scalar phases.
      Hence $\tilde\sigma_\gamma(n;k,b_2)$ equals the number of
      $g\in\mathrm{Shell}_n$ whose label $a(g)+k \bmod q$ already occurred
      among $\{a(h)+k \bmod q : h\in\mathrm{Shell}_{<n}\}$.
      The multiset $\{a(g) \bmod q : g\in\mathrm{Shell}_m\}$ is a property
      of the abstract Cayley graph alone --- the same for every $\gamma$
      and every $k$ --- and translation by the constant $k$ is a bijection
      of $\ZqZ$, hence preserves every collision and repetition statistic
      of that multiset exactly.
      Therefore $\tilde\sigma_\gamma(n;k,b_2)$ is exactly independent of
      $\gamma$, $k$, and $b_2$: it equals a single function
      $\tilde\sigma(n;q)$ of $n$ and $q$ alone, for every character and
      every choice of initial block parameters.
      Equation~\eqref{eq:block_indep} follows by evaluating this function
      at $(c,b_1,b_2)$ and $(c',b_1',b_2')$.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[What is structurally robust --- and what is not shown]
Theorem~\ref{thm:identity} and Corollary~\ref{cor:structural_equiv}
together establish exact pointwise equality of $\tilde\sigma$ across
\emph{every} block, not only conjugate ones.
This is stronger than what the original draft claimed, but it is a
statement about this paper's own toy model
(equation~\eqref{eq:weil_def}), and it is exactly this strength that
undercuts the interpretive use made of it in
Section~\ref{sec:why_pair}: a fact holding between arbitrary,
non-conjugate blocks cannot, by itself, be evidence that conjugate
pairs specifically are the fibres of a projection~$\Pi$ organised
around the involution $c\leftrightarrow q-c$.
Whatever such fibres are, this identity does not distinguish them from
any other pairing of blocks.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{remark}[Numerical confirmation --- corrected attribution]
The original draft's worked example, for $(c{=}1,c{=}60)$ at $q=61$
with blocks $(b_1{=}27,b_2{=}2)$ and $(b_1{=}3,b_2{=}30)$, used
$b_1\neq b_1'$ ($27\neq3$): it therefore already tested
Corollary~\ref{cor:structural_equiv}'s general case, not
Theorem~\ref{thm:identity}'s restricted one, contrary to what it
claimed to confirm.
A script reproducing this example, and independently testing
equation~\eqref{eq:block_indep} between genuinely unrelated
(non-conjugate) blocks at $q=29$ out to $n=20$ (well past the point
where shell growth wraps around $q$), is included in
\texttt{code/o17\_block\_independence\_check.py}
(\texttt{python3 o17\_block\_independence\_check.py}); all tested
pairs agree exactly, as equation~\eqref{eq:block_indep} requires.
    \end{remark}

  \section{The factor \texorpdfstring{$r(c,q)$}{r(c,q)} is a normalisation artefact}
  \label{sec:r_artefact}

  \subsection{Normalisation, within this paper's own model}

    The original draft asserted that ``the pipeline observable $\sigma_c(n)$
    reported in O12--O15'' is a normalised form of the raw count
    $\tilde\sigma_c(n)$.
    This is not established: no argument is given here, or anywhere in
    this paper, that the real O12/O13 pipeline's $\Sigma_n^{(c_1,c_2,c_3)}$
    decomposes this way, and Section~\ref{sec:why_pair} withdraws the
    identification of this paper's model with that pipeline entirely.
    What follows is instead a statement purely internal to this paper's
    own toy model: \emph{if} a normalised observable is defined, within
    this model, as
    \begin{equation}
      \sigma_\gamma(n;\, k, b_2)
      \;:=\; \frac{\tilde\sigma_\gamma(n;\, k, b_2)}{D(\gamma, k, b_2, n)}
      \label{eq:sigma_normalised}
    \end{equation}
    for some denominator $D$ depending on the block parameters and shell
    index, \emph{then} the following holds.

  \subsection{Consequence of block-independence}

    By Corollary~\ref{cor:structural_equiv}, $\tilde\sigma_\gamma(n;k,b_2)$
    is exactly independent of $\gamma$, $k$, and $b_2$.
    Consequently, for \emph{any} such normalised observable
    satisfying~\eqref{eq:sigma_normalised}, all of its block-dependence ---
    whatever block-dependence it exhibits --- resides entirely in the
    denominator $D$:
    \begin{equation}
      \sigma_{\gamma'}(n;\,k',b_2') \big/ \sigma_\gamma(n;\,k,b_2)
      \;=\; D(\gamma,k,b_2,n) \big/ D(\gamma',k',b_2',n),
      \label{eq:r_ratio}
    \end{equation}
    since the $\tilde\sigma$ factors cancel identically.
    In particular, restricted to conjugate pairs $\gamma'=q-\gamma$, this
    recovers O16's ratio $r(c,q) = D(c,k,b_2,n)/D(q-c,k',b_2',n)$.

    \begin{proposition}[$r$ is a normalisation artefact --- proof corrected]
      \label{prop:r_artefact}
      Within the setting of equation~\eqref{eq:sigma_normalised}, if $r$
      (or its generalisation~\eqref{eq:r_ratio} between arbitrary blocks)
      varies across choices of $(k,b_2)$ or $(k',b_2')$ at fixed
      $(\gamma,\gamma')$, that variation is entirely a property of $D$: it
      cannot originate in $\tilde\sigma$, which
      Corollary~\ref{cor:structural_equiv} shows is exactly the same
      function for every block.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      Immediate from equation~\eqref{eq:r_ratio} and
      Corollary~\ref{cor:structural_equiv}: the right-hand side of
      \eqref{eq:r_ratio} is the \emph{only} place block-parameter
      dependence can enter, since the left-hand side's numerator and
      denominator both factor through the single, block-independent
      function $\tilde\sigma(n;q)$.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[What changed from the original draft]
The original draft's Proposition (there stated as ``$r$ depends on
block parameters, not on $(c,q)$'') was supported by a specific numeric
example ($q=29$, character $c=11$ with two block instances, paired
against $c=18$, giving $r=2$ or $r=1$ depending on which instance was
used).
This repository has never carried code computing $\sigma_c(n)$, $r$, or
$D$: the example was not reproducible and is withdrawn.
The proposition's \emph{conclusion} survives, but for a different, and
now unconditional, reason: it is a direct logical consequence of
Corollary~\ref{cor:structural_equiv}, needing no numeric example at all,
proved above.
    \end{remark}

  \subsection{Consequences, and their scope}

    \begin{corollary}[Inertness of $r$ for the exponent --- conditional]
      \label{cor:r_inert}
      \emph{If}, in addition to~\eqref{eq:sigma_normalised}, the ratio $r$
      of equation~\eqref{eq:r_ratio} happens to be independent of the
      shell index~$n$, \emph{then}
      $\log\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n) = \log r + \log\sigma_c(n) + \log\sigma_{q-c}(n)$,
      so $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}} = \delta_c + \delta_{q-c}$: the value of
      $r$ does not affect the exponent.
      This is the same conditional statement as O16's
      equation~(doubling), restated here; whether any sourced $D$
      satisfies the hypothesis is not addressed by this paper.
    \end{corollary}

    \begin{remark}[$r$ is gauge-like, within this model]
      \label{rem:r_gauge}
      Within the setting of equation~\eqref{eq:sigma_normalised}, $r$ is
      not intrinsic to the representation: it is entirely a property of
      the normalisation $D$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:r_artefact}), and, when
      Corollary~\ref{cor:r_inert}'s hypothesis holds, leaves
      $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}$ invariant.
      Both statements are internal to this paper's own model and are not
      shown to describe the real O12/O13 pipeline's actual normalisation,
      whose functional form this paper does not examine.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{corollary}[Consistency with O16 Remark~6.4's own withdrawal]
      \label{cor:remark_closure}
      O16's original speculation that $r(c,q)$ might originate from
      quadratic Gauss sums or character multiplicities was itself already
      withdrawn by O16's own corrective revision~\cite{Beau2026a20} (its
      remark on the amplitude factor: the speculation depended on O16's
      proposition on conjugate sigma-profile proportionality, withdrawn
      there for lack of a reproducible source), on grounds independent of
      this paper.
      This paper's Proposition~\ref{prop:r_artefact} reaches a compatible
      conclusion --- $r$ is determined entirely by the normalisation~$D$,
      not by any representation-theoretic quantity --- but within its own
      model and by a different route (block-independence of the raw
      count), not as a new closure of an O16 question that O16 had
      already closed itself.
      This conclusion is scoped to the model of
      equation~\eqref{eq:sigma_normalised}; it is not established for the
      real pipeline's actual, unexamined normalisation.
    \end{corollary}

    \begin{corollary}[The structural invariant --- strengthened, and reinterpreted]
      \label{cor:invariant}
      The raw redundancy count $\tilde\sigma$ is exactly block-independent
      (Corollary~\ref{cor:structural_equiv}): $\delta$ computed from
      $\tilde\sigma$ alone is the \emph{same} for every block, not only
      conjugate ones.
      Whether the \emph{normalised, reported} exponent $\delta_c$ varies
      across blocks at all --- as the real O-series measurements show it
      empirically does --- therefore depends entirely on properties of the
      normalisation $D$ that this paper's toy model leaves unspecified.
      Equation~\eqref{eq:sigma_normalised} is a hypothesis about the shape
      of that normalisation, not a description of it; nothing here derives
      $D$ or shows it exists for the real pipeline.
    \end{corollary}

  \section{Withdrawn: interpretive claims about \texorpdfstring{$\Pi$}{Pi} and O14--O15}
  \label{sec:why_pair}

  The original draft's ``hierarchy of observables,'' ``why fibre-level is
  the correct class,'' and ``requalification of O14--O15'' subsections are
  withdrawn in full.
  Two independent reasons apply.

  \paragraph{The pipeline identification is not established.}
    Everything below rested on treating this paper's toy model
    (Section~\ref{sec:identity}) as a derivation of ``the observable class
    dictated by $\Pi$'' for the real O12/O13 pipeline.
    No argument anywhere in this paper connects the toy representation of
    equation~\eqref{eq:weil_def} --- whose fingerprint vectors are single
    \emph{position}-basis vectors $e_k$ --- to the real pipeline's actual
    fingerprint construction, whose vectors are single
    \emph{frequency}-basis vectors indexed by a generic triple
    $(c_1,c_2,c_3)$, not a scalar~$c$.
    The claim that conjugate pairs ``are the fibres of $\Pi$ as measured
    by the real pipeline'' is accordingly withdrawn, along with the
    consequent ``requalification'' of O14--O15 as correct-but-incomplete
    block-level analyses.

  \paragraph{The argument, taken on its own terms, proves too much.}
    Independently of the pipeline question, the original draft's central
    step --- ``$\rho_c$ and $\rho_{q-c}$ produce identical Gram-Schmidt
    dynamics, indicating indistinguishable observable content, hence the
    same fibre of $\Pi$'' --- no longer supports what it is used for.
    Corollary~\ref{cor:structural_equiv} (Section~\ref{sec:identity})
    shows that \emph{every} pair of blocks, conjugate or not, produces
    identical raw Gram-Schmidt dynamics in this toy model.
    Applying the original draft's own inference rule (identical dynamics
    $\Rightarrow$ same fibre) to that stronger fact would place
    \emph{every} block in a single common fibre, collapsing the fibre
    structure entirely --- not the pairwise structure $\{c,q-c\}$ the
    argument was meant to establish.
    Since the original draft did not intend or defend that collapsed
    conclusion, its inference rule cannot be sound as stated; the
    ``identical dynamics'' fact, on its own, does not license any
    conclusion about which blocks share a fibre.

  \paragraph{O15's citation, corrected.}
    The withdrawn ``O15 Corollary~4.8 establishes that no reweighting of
    block data recovers the fibre-level exponent'' cited an unconditional
    no-go that O15's own corrective revision found false as originally
    stated (explicit counterexample: asymmetric weight concentration
    exceeds $\dhat_{\mathrm{exact}}$).
    The corrected corollary is narrower --- specific dynamical weights,
    hypothesis since verified at one of the five tested primes
    ($q=151$), still open at the other four, bounding against
    $\dhat_{\mathrm{exact}}$ --- and in any case concerns the real
    pipeline's block-level observable, not this paper's toy model, to
    which it was never shown to apply.
    The ``complementarity of O15 and O17'' remark, and the ``O15 closes
    the block-level programme; O17 opens the fibre-level programme''
    framing, depend on both withdrawn premises and are withdrawn with
    them.

  No claim about $\Pi$'s fibre structure, about O14--O15's completeness,
  or about the physical status of conjugate pairs is asserted in this
  revision.
  What Section~\ref{sec:identity} establishes stands on its own, entirely
  independently of this section's withdrawn interpretive superstructure.

  \section{Summary and open directions}
  \label{sec:summary}

  \subsection{What is established}

    \begin{enumerate}
      \item Within this paper's own toy Weil-block model
      (equation~\eqref{eq:weil_def}), the raw Gram-Schmidt redundancy
      count $\tilde\sigma$ is \emph{exactly} independent of the
      character~$c$ and of both initial-condition parameters $(b_1,b_2)$
      --- not only equal for conjugate pairs, but identical for
      \emph{any} two blocks whatsoever
      (Theorem~\ref{thm:identity}, Corollary~\ref{cor:structural_equiv}).
      This is stronger than, and supersedes, the original draft's
      permutation-isomorphism claim.

      \item This block-independence is a purely combinatorial fact about
      the underlying Cayley graph (translation-invariance of shell-label
      collisions), proved without appeal to the conjugation identity
      $\rho_{q-c}=\overline{\rho_c}$, although that identity remains
      independently true and is used in Theorem~\ref{thm:identity}'s own
      proof for the restricted case.

      \item If a normalised observable is defined, within this paper's
      model, as $\sigma_\gamma := \tilde\sigma/D$ for some denominator
      $D$ (equation~\eqref{eq:sigma_normalised}), then any block-dependence
      it exhibits comes entirely from $D$
      (Proposition~\ref{prop:r_artefact}) --- a direct consequence of
      item~1, not an independently verified empirical claim.
      This is scoped strictly to this paper's own model; whether it says
      anything about the real O12/O13 pipeline's actual normalisation is
      not addressed.

      \item \textbf{Withdrawn.}
      The original draft's claims that the pair observable is the
      correct unit of admissibility because conjugate pairs are the
      fibres of $\Pi$, and that O14--O15 are ``correct-but-incomplete''
      block-level analyses requalified by this paper, are withdrawn in
      full (Section~\ref{sec:why_pair}): the pipeline identification
      needed for either claim is not established, and item~1's stronger
      form shows the ``identical dynamics $\Rightarrow$ same fibre''
      argument, taken literally, would place \emph{every} block in one
      fibre --- not the conclusion the original draft drew from it.
    \end{enumerate}

  \subsection{Open directions}

    \paragraph{Whether this toy model says anything about the real pipeline.}
      This paper's model (equation~\eqref{eq:weil_def}) represents each
      block by a single \emph{position}-basis vector; the real O12/O13
      pipeline's exact fingerprints are single \emph{frequency}-basis
      vectors indexed by a generic triple $(c_1,c_2,c_3)$.
      Whether a corresponding block-independence result holds for the
      real pipeline's raw (pre-normalisation) redundancy count, and if
      so what its normalisation $D$ actually is, is open and not
      addressed by this paper.

    \paragraph{The fibre structure of $\Pi$.}
      The identification of any specific pairing or grouping of blocks
      with the fibres of the non-injective projection~$\Pi$ remains
      entirely open.
      Item~1's stronger block-independence result shows that ``identical
      raw dynamics'' cannot by itself be the criterion, since it holds
      too broadly; a different, genuinely selective criterion would be
      needed to justify grouping specifically conjugate blocks (or any
      other specific grouping) into fibres.

    \paragraph{Upper bound of the phenomenological window --- unreproducible, withdrawn.}
      The original draft cited ``numerical exploration (post-O16)''
      showing a spectrum of $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}$ values bounded near
      $7.4$ and extending above $10.6$.
      This repository has never carried code or data supporting this
      claim; it is withdrawn for lack of a reproducible source.
