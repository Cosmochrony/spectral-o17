  \begin{abstract}
    \vspace{0.5em}
    Within a scalar Weil-representation toy model on $\mathbb{C}^q$, the
    raw Gram-Schmidt redundancy count $\tilde\sigma_c(n;b_1,b_2)$ at BFS
    shell~$n$ is \emph{exactly} independent of the central
    character~$c \in \Fq$ and of both initial-condition
    parameters~$(b_1,b_2)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:identity},
    Corollary~\ref{cor:structural_equiv}): not only equal for conjugate
    characters $c$ and $q-c$, but identical for \emph{any} two blocks
    whatsoever.
    The proof is purely combinatorial --- translation-invariance of
    shell-label collisions on the underlying Cayley graph --- and needs
    no conjugation identity.
    A direct consequence: for any normalised observable
    $\sigma_c := \tilde\sigma_c/D$ defined within this model, all of its
    block-dependence resides entirely in the normalisation~$D$
    (Proposition~\ref{prop:r_artefact}); if that dependence happens to be
    shell-index-independent, the pair-level exponent adds without any
    contribution from~$D$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:r_inert}).

    These results are scoped strictly to this paper's own toy model,
    whose block fingerprints are single \emph{position}-basis vectors
    $e_k$ (equation~\eqref{eq:v0}).
    The real O12/O13 pipeline's exact fingerprints are single
    \emph{frequency}-basis vectors indexed by a generic triple
    $(c_1,c_2,c_3)$, not a scalar~$c$; no map between the two index
    spaces is given here, so none of the results above is asserted for
    that pipeline.
    Because block-independence in this model holds for \emph{any} two
    blocks and not only conjugate ones, it cannot by itself be a
    criterion for grouping specifically conjugate blocks into fibres of a
    projection~$\Pi$ (Section~\ref{sec:why_pair}): applied uniformly, it
    would place every block in one fibre.
  \end{abstract}

  \paragraph{Keywords.}
    Weil representation, conjugate Weil blocks, Gram--Schmidt redundancy, block-independence, Heisenberg Cayley graphs,
    scalar model

  \section{The open question}
  \label{sec:problem}

  O16 proposes a pair-level observable
  $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n) := \sigma_c(n)\,\sigma_{q-c}(n)$, built from
  the exact conjugation identity $\rho_{q-c}=\overline{\rho_c}$, as a
  candidate fibre-level unit of admissibility~\cite{Beau2026a20}.
  That identification rests on two separate hypotheses --- that the
  non-injective projection~$\Pi$ carries a parity involution, and that
  this involution is realised, in the Weil representation, as
  $c \mapsto q-c$ --- neither derived from first principles.
  Independently of those hypotheses, two purely mathematical questions
  about the raw dynamics underlying the proposal are open:

  \begin{itemize}
    \item[(Q1)] Why is the raw Gram-Schmidt dynamics identical for
    $\rho_c$ and $\rho_{q-c}$?
    Is this a consequence of $\rho_{q-c} = \overline{\rho_c}$,
    or does it require additional structure?

    \item[(Q2)] What is the structural origin of the
    constant~$r(c,q)$ satisfying
    $\sigma_{q-c}(n) = r(c,q)\,\sigma_c(n)$ for all $n \geq 1$?
    Is it an arithmetic invariant of $(c,q)$, or a pipeline artefact?
  \end{itemize}

  This paper answers both questions, within an explicitly defined toy
  model (Section~\ref{sec:identity}), for that model alone: as
  Section~\ref{sec:identity} shows, the answer to (Q1)--(Q2) in this toy
  model holds for \emph{any} two blocks, conjugate or not, a fact whose
  scope for the fibre question is examined in
  Section~\ref{sec:why_pair}.

  \section{Identity of raw Gram-Schmidt dynamics}
  \label{sec:identity}

  \subsection{Setup}

    Let $q$ be an odd prime.
    The Weil representation $\rho_c$ of $\Heis(\ZqZ)$ at central
    character~$c \in \Fq$ acts on $\mathbb{C}^q$ by:
    \begin{equation}
      [\rho_c(a,b,t)\,\psi](x) \;=\; \omega^{c(bx + t)}\,\psi(x-a),
      \qquad \omega = e^{2\pi i/q}.
      \label{eq:weil_def}
    \end{equation}
    A block in \emph{this paper's own toy model} --- not shown to
    coincide with the real O12/O13 pipeline's block structure, a generic
    triple $(c_1,c_2,c_3)$ with frequency-domain fingerprints
    (Section~\ref{sec:why_pair}) --- is specified by a triple
    $(c, b_1, b_2) \in \Fq \times (\ZqZ)^2$, with initial fingerprint
    vector:
    \begin{equation}
      v_0(c, b_1, b_2) \;:=\; \rho_c(b_1, b_2, 0)\,e_0
      \;=\; \omega^{c\,b_2 b_1}\,e_{b_1},
      \label{eq:v0}
    \end{equation}
    where $e_k$ denotes the $k$-th standard basis vector of $\mathbb{C}^q$.

    The Gram-Schmidt redundancy count at BFS shell~$n$ for this block is:
    \begin{multline}
      \tilde\sigma_c(n;\, b_1, b_2)
      \;:=\; \bigl|\bigl\{g \in \mathrm{Shell}_n :
      \rho_c(g)\,v_0(c,b_1,b_2) \\
      \in \mathrm{span}\bigl\{\rho_c(h)\,v_0 : h \in \mathrm{Shell}_{<n}\bigr\}\bigr\}\bigr|.
      \label{eq:raw_sigma}
    \end{multline}
    This is the \emph{raw} count: the number of Weil images at shell~$n$
    that are already in the Gram-Schmidt span accumulated over
    shells~$0, \ldots, n-1$.

  \subsection{Main results}

    The analysis splits into two levels: an exact equality under a support
    condition, and block-independence for arbitrary block parameters.

    \begin{theorem}[Identity of raw dynamics, restricted form]
      \label{thm:identity}
      Let $q$ be an odd prime and $c \in \Fq$.
      Suppose the two blocks share the same initial support, i.e.\ $b_1 = b_1'$.
      Then:
      \begin{equation}
        \tilde\sigma_{q-c}(n;\, b_1, b_2')
        \;=\; \tilde\sigma_c(n;\, b_1, b_2)
        \qquad \text{for all } n \geq 0.
        \label{eq:raw_identity}
      \end{equation}
    \end{theorem}

    \begin{proof}
      From equation~\eqref{eq:v0}, both initial vectors have support $\{b_1\}$:
      \[
        v_0(c,b_1,b_2) = \omega^{c\,b_2 b_1}\,e_{b_1},
        \qquad
        v_0(q-c,b_1,b_2') = \omega^{(q-c)\,b_2' b_1}\,e_{b_1}.
      \]
      These differ only by an overall unimodular scalar.
      The conjugation identity $\rho_{q-c} = \overline{\rho_c}$ gives,
      for every $g \in \Heis(\ZqZ)$:
      \[
        \rho_{q-c}(g)\,v_0(q-c,b_1,b_2')
        \;=\; \lambda\,\overline{\rho_c(g)\,v_0(c,b_1,b_2)},
      \]
      where $\lambda \in \mathbb{C}$ is a phase independent of~$g$.
      Since Gram-Schmidt orthogonalisation depends only on the
      linear independence structure of vectors, and since complex
      conjugation preserves this structure
      ($|\langle \bar u, \bar v \rangle| = |\langle u, v \rangle|$),
      the redundancy count of
      $\{\rho_{q-c}(g)\,v_0 : g \in \mathrm{Shell}_{\leq n}\}$
      equals that of
      $\{\rho_c(g)\,v_0 : g \in \mathrm{Shell}_{\leq n}\}$
      at every shell.
      Moreover, complex conjugation preserves linear independence relations
      over $\mathbb{C}$, ensuring that the span structure generated by the
      group orbit is unchanged: a set of vectors is linearly independent
      if and only if its complex conjugate is.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[Scope of Theorem~\ref{thm:identity}]
      \label{rem:scope}
      Exact pointwise equality of the raw counts holds even when
      $b_1 \neq b_1'$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:structural_equiv} below), for a
      reason independent of the conjugation identity used in
      Theorem~\ref{thm:identity}'s proof.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{corollary}[Block-independence of the raw redundancy count]
      \label{cor:structural_equiv}
      For \emph{any} two blocks $(c,b_1,b_2)$ and $(c',b_1',b_2')$ with
      $c,c' \in \Fq$ arbitrary (not necessarily conjugate) and
      $(b_1,b_2),(b_1',b_2') \in (\ZqZ)^2$ arbitrary,
      \begin{equation}
        \tilde\sigma_{c'}(n;\,b_1',b_2') \;=\; \tilde\sigma_c(n;\,b_1,b_2)
        \qquad \text{exactly, for all } n \geq 0.
        \label{eq:block_indep}
      \end{equation}
      In particular $\delta_{c'} = \delta_c$ for any $c,c'$, not only for
      conjugate pairs.
    \end{corollary}

    \begin{proof}
      From equation~\eqref{eq:weil_def}, for any character $\gamma\in\Fq$
      and any $k\in\ZqZ$,
      \[
        \rho_\gamma(a,b,t)\,e_k \;=\; \omega^{\gamma(b(a+k)+t)}\,e_{a+k}:
      \]
      applying $\rho_\gamma(a,b,t)$ to the indicator $e_k$ produces a
      \emph{single} basis vector, supported at $a+k$, scaled by a
      unimodular phase.
      By induction on word length (using the Heisenberg group law, whose
      first coordinate is additive: the first coordinate of $g\cdot g'$ is
      the sum of the first coordinates of $g$ and $g'$), for any
      $g=(a,b,t)$ reachable in the Cayley graph,
      \[
        \rho_\gamma(g)\,v_0(\gamma,k,b_2)
        \;=\; \varphi(g,\gamma,k,b_2)\;e_{a+k}
      \]
      for some unimodular $\varphi$ depending on $g,\gamma,k,b_2$ but
      \emph{not} affecting which basis vector is produced: the support is
      exactly $a+k \bmod q$, determined by $g$'s own first coordinate $a$
      and the shift $k$, and is independent of $\gamma$ and of $b_2$
      entirely.
      Every vector in the orbit $\{\rho_\gamma(g)v_0 : g\in\mathrm{Shell}_{\leq n}\}$
      is therefore a nonzero scalar multiple of a standard basis vector.
      Gram-Schmidt redundancy for a family of vectors that are each
      (nonzero scalar)$\times$(basis vector) depends only on which basis
      indices repeat: two such vectors are linearly dependent iff they
      share the same index, and linearly independent iff they do not,
      regardless of their scalar phases.
      Hence $\tilde\sigma_\gamma(n;k,b_2)$ equals the number of
      $g\in\mathrm{Shell}_n$ whose label $a(g)+k \bmod q$ already occurred
      among $\{a(h)+k \bmod q : h\in\mathrm{Shell}_{<n}\}$.
      The multiset $\{a(g) \bmod q : g\in\mathrm{Shell}_m\}$ is a property
      of the abstract Cayley graph alone --- the same for every $\gamma$
      and every $k$ --- and translation by the constant $k$ is a bijection
      of $\ZqZ$, hence preserves every collision and repetition statistic
      of that multiset exactly.
      Therefore $\tilde\sigma_\gamma(n;k,b_2)$ is exactly independent of
      $\gamma$, $k$, and $b_2$: it equals a single function
      $\tilde\sigma(n;q)$ of $n$ and $q$ alone, for every character and
      every choice of initial block parameters.
      Equation~\eqref{eq:block_indep} follows by evaluating this function
      at $(c,b_1,b_2)$ and $(c',b_1',b_2')$.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[What is structurally robust --- and what is not shown]
Theorem~\ref{thm:identity} and Corollary~\ref{cor:structural_equiv}
together establish exact pointwise equality of $\tilde\sigma$ across
\emph{every} block, not only conjugate ones.
This is a statement about this paper's own toy model
(equation~\eqref{eq:weil_def}), and it is exactly this strength that
limits its interpretive use in Section~\ref{sec:why_pair}: a fact
holding between arbitrary, non-conjugate blocks cannot, by itself, be
evidence that conjugate pairs specifically are the fibres of a
projection~$\Pi$ organised around the involution $c\leftrightarrow q-c$.
Whatever such fibres are, this identity does not distinguish them from
any other pairing of blocks.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{remark}[Numerical confirmation]
A script verifying equation~\eqref{eq:block_indep} --- reproducing the
worked example $(c{=}1,c{=}60)$ at $q=61$ with blocks
$(b_1{=}27,b_2{=}2)$ and $(b_1{=}3,b_2{=}30)$, and independently testing
the identity between genuinely unrelated (non-conjugate) blocks at
$q=29$ out to $n=20$ (well past the point where shell growth wraps
around $q$) --- is included in the accompanying verification script,
\texttt{code/o17\_block\allowbreak\_independence\allowbreak\_check.py},
run as \texttt{python3 o17\_block\allowbreak\_independence\allowbreak\_check.py}.
All tested pairs agree exactly, as equation~\eqref{eq:block_indep}
requires.
    \end{remark}

  \section{The factor \texorpdfstring{$r(c,q)$}{r(c,q)} is a normalisation artefact}
  \label{sec:r_artefact}

  \subsection{Normalisation, within this paper's own model}

    No argument is given here, or anywhere in this paper, that the real
    O12/O13 pipeline's $\Sigma_n^{(c_1,c_2,c_3)}$ decomposes into a raw
    count times a normalisation; what follows is a statement purely
    internal to this paper's own toy model:
    \emph{if} a normalised observable is defined, within this model, as
    \begin{equation}
      \sigma_\gamma(n;\, k, b_2)
      \;:=\; \frac{\tilde\sigma_\gamma(n;\, k, b_2)}{D(\gamma, k, b_2, n)}
      \label{eq:sigma_normalised}
    \end{equation}
    for some denominator $D$ depending on the block parameters and shell
    index, \emph{then} the following holds.

  \subsection{Consequence of block-independence}

    By Corollary~\ref{cor:structural_equiv}, $\tilde\sigma_\gamma(n;k,b_2)$
    is exactly independent of $\gamma$, $k$, and $b_2$.
    Consequently, for \emph{any} such normalised observable
    satisfying~\eqref{eq:sigma_normalised}, all of its block-dependence ---
    whatever block-dependence it exhibits --- resides entirely in the
    denominator $D$:
    \begin{equation}
      \sigma_{\gamma'}(n;\,k',b_2') \big/ \sigma_\gamma(n;\,k,b_2)
      \;=\; D(\gamma,k,b_2,n) \big/ D(\gamma',k',b_2',n),
      \label{eq:r_ratio}
    \end{equation}
    since the $\tilde\sigma$ factors cancel identically.
    In particular, restricted to conjugate pairs $\gamma'=q-\gamma$, this
    recovers O16's ratio $r(c,q) = D(c,k,b_2,n)/D(q-c,k',b_2',n)$.

    \begin{proposition}[$r$ is a normalisation artefact]
      \label{prop:r_artefact}
      Within the setting of equation~\eqref{eq:sigma_normalised}, if $r$
      (or its generalisation~\eqref{eq:r_ratio} between arbitrary blocks)
      varies across choices of $(k,b_2)$ or $(k',b_2')$ at fixed
      $(\gamma,\gamma')$, that variation is entirely a property of $D$: it
      cannot originate in $\tilde\sigma$, which
      Corollary~\ref{cor:structural_equiv} shows is exactly the same
      function for every block.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      Immediate from equation~\eqref{eq:r_ratio} and
      Corollary~\ref{cor:structural_equiv}: the right-hand side of
      \eqref{eq:r_ratio} is the \emph{only} place block-parameter
      dependence can enter, since the left-hand side's numerator and
      denominator both factor through the single, block-independent
      function $\tilde\sigma(n;q)$.
    \end{proof}

  \subsection{Consequences, and their scope}

    \begin{corollary}[Inertness of $r$ for the exponent --- conditional]
      \label{cor:r_inert}
      \emph{If}, in addition to~\eqref{eq:sigma_normalised}, the ratio $r$
      of equation~\eqref{eq:r_ratio} happens to be independent of the
      shell index~$n$, \emph{then}
      $\log\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n) = \log r + \log\sigma_c(n) + \log\sigma_{q-c}(n)$,
      so $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}} = \delta_c + \delta_{q-c}$: the value of
      $r$ does not affect the exponent.
      Whether any sourced $D$ satisfies this hypothesis is not addressed
      by this paper.
    \end{corollary}

    \begin{remark}[$r$ is gauge-like, within this model]
      \label{rem:r_gauge}
      Within the setting of equation~\eqref{eq:sigma_normalised}, $r$ is
      not intrinsic to the representation: it is entirely a property of
      the normalisation $D$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:r_artefact}), and, when
      Corollary~\ref{cor:r_inert}'s hypothesis holds, leaves
      $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}$ invariant.
      Both statements are internal to this paper's own model and are not
      shown to describe the real O12/O13 pipeline's actual normalisation,
      whose functional form this paper does not examine.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{corollary}[The structural invariant]
      \label{cor:invariant}
      The raw redundancy count $\tilde\sigma$ is exactly block-independent
      (Corollary~\ref{cor:structural_equiv}): $\delta$ computed from
      $\tilde\sigma$ alone is the \emph{same} for every block, not only
      conjugate ones.
      Whether the \emph{normalised, reported} exponent $\delta_c$ varies
      across blocks at all --- as the real O-series measurements show it
      empirically does --- therefore depends entirely on properties of the
      normalisation $D$ that this paper's toy model leaves unspecified.
      Equation~\eqref{eq:sigma_normalised} is a hypothesis about the shape
      of that normalisation, not a description of it; nothing here derives
      $D$ or shows it exists for the real pipeline.
    \end{corollary}

  \section{Scope: block-independence and the fibre structure of \texorpdfstring{$\Pi$}{Pi}}
  \label{sec:why_pair}

  \paragraph{Relation to the O12/O13 pipeline.}
    This paper's toy model (Section~\ref{sec:identity}) is not shown to
    coincide with the real O12/O13 pipeline.
    The toy representation of equation~\eqref{eq:weil_def} has fingerprint
    vectors that are single \emph{position}-basis vectors $e_k$; the real
    pipeline's actual fingerprint construction uses single
    \emph{frequency}-basis vectors indexed by a generic triple
    $(c_1,c_2,c_3)$, not a scalar~$c$.
    No argument in this paper connects the two, so none of the results of
    Sections~\ref{sec:identity}--\ref{sec:r_artefact} is asserted for the
    real pipeline.

  \paragraph{What block-independence can and cannot support about fibres.}
    Corollary~\ref{cor:structural_equiv} shows that \emph{every} pair of
    blocks, conjugate or not, produces identical raw Gram-Schmidt dynamics
    in this toy model.
    A criterion of the form ``identical raw dynamics $\Rightarrow$ same
    fibre of~$\Pi$'' would, applied to that fact, place \emph{every} block
    in a single common fibre, collapsing the fibre structure entirely ---
    not the pairwise structure $\{c,q-c\}$ that motivates O16's
    proposal~\cite{Beau2026a20}.
    Identical raw dynamics, on its own, therefore does not license any
    conclusion about which blocks specifically share a fibre; a
    genuinely selective criterion, distinguishing conjugate pairs from
    other block pairings, remains to be supplied.

  \section{Summary and open directions}
  \label{sec:summary}

  \subsection{What is established}

    \begin{enumerate}
      \item Within this paper's own toy Weil-block model
      (equation~\eqref{eq:weil_def}), the raw Gram-Schmidt redundancy
      count $\tilde\sigma$ is \emph{exactly} independent of the
      character~$c$ and of both initial-condition parameters $(b_1,b_2)$
      --- not only equal for conjugate pairs, but identical for
      \emph{any} two blocks whatsoever
      (Theorem~\ref{thm:identity}, Corollary~\ref{cor:structural_equiv}).

      \item This block-independence is a purely combinatorial fact about
      the underlying Cayley graph (translation-invariance of shell-label
      collisions), proved without appeal to the conjugation identity
      $\rho_{q-c}=\overline{\rho_c}$, although that identity remains
      independently true and is used in Theorem~\ref{thm:identity}'s own
      proof for the restricted case.

      \item If a normalised observable is defined, within this paper's
      model, as $\sigma_\gamma := \tilde\sigma/D$ for some denominator
      $D$ (equation~\eqref{eq:sigma_normalised}), then any block-dependence
      it exhibits comes entirely from $D$
      (Proposition~\ref{prop:r_artefact}) --- a direct consequence of
      item~1, not an independently verified empirical claim.
      This is scoped strictly to this paper's own model; whether it says
      anything about the real O12/O13 pipeline's actual normalisation is
      not addressed.
    \end{enumerate}

  \subsection{Open directions}

    \paragraph{Whether this toy model says anything about the real pipeline.}
      This paper's model (equation~\eqref{eq:weil_def}) represents each
      block by a single \emph{position}-basis vector; the real O12/O13
      pipeline's exact fingerprints are single \emph{frequency}-basis
      vectors indexed by a generic triple $(c_1,c_2,c_3)$.
      Whether a corresponding block-independence result holds for the
      real pipeline's raw (pre-normalisation) redundancy count, and if
      so what its normalisation $D$ actually is, is open and not
      addressed by this paper.

    \paragraph{The fibre structure of $\Pi$.}
      The identification of any specific pairing or grouping of blocks
      with the fibres of the non-injective projection~$\Pi$ remains
      entirely open.
      The block-independence result of Section~\ref{sec:identity} shows
      that ``identical raw dynamics'' cannot by itself be the criterion,
      since it holds too broadly; a different, genuinely selective
      criterion would be needed to justify grouping specifically
      conjugate blocks (or any other specific grouping) into fibres.
